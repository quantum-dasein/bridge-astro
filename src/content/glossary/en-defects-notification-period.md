The Defects Notification Period (DNP) is the period after taking over of the Works during which the Employer may notify defects and the Contractor must remedy them. In the FIDIC 2017 edition it is dealt with in Clause 11. In practice it is often called the defects liability period.

> **In brief:** The DNP starts on the taking-over date. Its length is stated in the Contract Data and, if not stated, is one year. After the last DNP expires the Engineer issues the Performance Certificate within 28 days (Sub-Clause 11.9): only that document confirms that the Contractor has performed its obligations.

## How the defects period works

- It starts on the completion date stated in the Taking-Over Certificate.
- Its length is as stated in the Contract Data; if not stated, one year.
- The Contractor completes outstanding work and remedies the defects the Employer has notified.
- After it ends the second half of the Retention Money is released (Sub-Clause 14.9).
- It closes with the Performance Certificate, within 28 days after the last DNP expires (Sub-Clause 11.9).

<aside class="bc-note"><strong>Look at your own contract first</strong><p>The time limits and procedure here follow the FIDIC 2017 General Conditions. The Contract Data and Particular Conditions may change them, so check the text of your own contract.</p></aside>

## Common mistakes

- The Taking-Over Certificate is taken for the end of the Contractor's obligations.
- A defect is reported orally, with no notice and no record of the date.
- The DNP is left blank in the Contract Data and the Parties count its end differently.
- The FIDIC defects period is confused with warranty periods under national law: the two may run in parallel.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is the Defects Notification Period?

As long as the Contract Data states. If no period is stated, under the FIDIC 2017 General Conditions it is one year.

### What is the Performance Certificate?

It is the certificate the Engineer issues after the defects period ends. It alone confirms that the Contractor has performed its obligations under the contract.
