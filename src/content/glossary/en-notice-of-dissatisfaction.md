A NOD (Notice of Dissatisfaction) is a written notice that a Party is dissatisfied with an Engineer's determination or a DAAB decision. It is a short document with great weight: if it is not given in time, the determination or decision becomes final and binding and can no longer be challenged.

> **In brief:** A NOD is given within 28 days: after an Engineer's determination under Sub-Clause 3.7 and after a DAAB decision under Clause 21. After a NOD against a DAAB decision the Parties have 28 days to attempt amicable settlement, then arbitration may follow. Missing the period makes the determination or decision final.

## How to give a NOD properly

1. Identify the document as a Notice of Dissatisfaction and refer to the Sub-Clause.
2. State what exactly you are dissatisfied with and, briefly, why.
3. Send it to the other Party and the Engineer (or the DAAB) by a method the contract accepts for notices.
4. Keep complying with the determination or decision: a NOD does not suspend it.

## Common mistakes

- The NOD is a few days late and the right to dispute is lost.
- Dissatisfaction is voiced in correspondence but not given as a notice.
- A Party gives a NOD and stops complying with the decision, creating a new breach of its own.
- The NOD covers only part of the decision although the Party later wants to dispute all of it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a NOD suspend compliance with the decision?

No. An Engineer's determination and a DAAB decision are complied with at once even if a NOD has been given. The notice only preserves the right to take the dispute further.

### Can a NOD be given against part of a decision?

Yes, but the rest then becomes final and binding. State clearly which parts you dispute.
