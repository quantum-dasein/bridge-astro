Retention Money is the **withheld sum** that the Employer deducts from each interim payment to secure itself against defects. In every payment cycle a share of the funds stays with the Employer rather than going to the Contractor; the return happens in stages — after Taking-Over and after the expiry of the DNP.

> **In brief:** Retention Money is the percentage withheld from interim payments under FIDIC: the standard limit is 5% up to 50% of the Works value and 0% thereafter; the first half is returned with the Taking-Over Certificate and the balance after the DNP. Missing the return claim and misreading the limit turns retention into a long-term, interest-free credit for the Employer.

## What retention money is: the withheld percentage and its purpose

Retention Money is the portion of each interim payment (a Statement under Sub-Clause 14.3) that the Employer withholds as security for the rectification of defects after Taking-Over. Under the General Conditions, unless the Particular Conditions have changed it, the withholding limit is expressed as a share of the accumulated value of Works executed: normally 5% up to 50% of the Contract price and 0% beyond that threshold, with a ceiling of 10% (the value from the Contract Data).

The purpose of the mechanism is to keep a “security” fund with the Employer for the duration of the DNP, so that the Contractor stays motivated to rectify defects. For the Contractor, retention is frozen working capital on which it finances the Works; for the Employer, it is a security instrument that requires no separate collateral. In multilateral development bank projects (World Bank, ADB, EBRD) standard Particular Conditions fix the limit and the return procedure; for the specific values, check the Contract Data and Particular Conditions in your own contract.

## Where retention is set out in FIDIC: book, edition and sub-clauses

The sub-clause numbers for the return and the limit of retention differ between editions: in 2017 the whole mechanism (limit and return) is consolidated in 14.9, in 1999 it is split across 14.10 (return) and 14.12 (limit); when reviewing a specific contract, always check the applicable edition and Contract Data. The key task for the Contractor is to fix the exact limit and the triggering date of the return in the Particular Conditions, rather than to rely on the standard. Details of the return after DNP are in the article on the Defects Notification Period.

## How retention money works in practice: limits and return timing

The mechanism works in three phases. Phase 1 — accumulation: the Contractor does not receive each interim payment in full but receives it minus the retention at the agreed limit. Phase 2 — first half: after the Taking-Over Certificate is issued, the Employer returns the first half of the accumulated retention to the Contractor. Phase 3 — balance: after the DNP expires and the Performance Certificate is issued, the second half is returned. The full return scheme and its link to Taking-Over are covered in the Taking-Over Certificate under FIDIC.

- **Retention limit** — standardly 5% up to 50% of the Works value and 0% thereafter (ceiling 10%; the exact value is in the Contract Data).
- **The first half of the retained amount** is returned after the Taking-Over Certificate is issued.
- **The balance** is returned after the DNP expires and the Performance Certificate is issued.
- **Currency of retention** — by default the same currency as the Contract; a different one only if stated in the Contract Data.
- A delay in the return gives the Contractor a right to **financial charges under Sub-Clause 14.8** (General Conditions); the parameters may be varied by the Particular Conditions.

<aside class="bc-note"><strong>Re-check the Contract Data</strong><p>All values — limit, currency, retention share and exceptions — are drawn from the Contract Data and Particular Conditions of the specific contract. If the Particular Conditions have altered the timing or limit, the standard 5%–0% and two-stage return may not apply.</p></aside>

## Typical contractor and employer mistakes in dealing with retention

- **Unverified limit in the Contract Data.** The Contractor calculates on the standard 5%, while the Particular Conditions set the limit at 10% or a fixed amount; the mismatch flows into the calculations and into a dispute.
- **Assuming the first half is automatic.** The return is tied to an event: the issue of the Taking-Over Certificate (Sub-Clause 14.9 in 2017; 14.10 in 1999). No event, no right to demand the return, even if the DNP is still running.
- **The Contractor does not request the Performance Certificate after DNP.** Without it, the second half of the retention is not returned; see Sub-Clause 11.9 on the Defects Notification Period.
- **Retention is not reflected in the cash flow.** The Contractor plans the payment of subcontracts and materials on the full payment amount, but in fact receives it minus the retention; on long projects this turns into months of free financing for the Employer.
- **The interest on financial charges for late return is not claimed.** If the return is delayed, the Contractor must explicitly request compensation under Sub-Clause 14.8; silence means lost money.
- **The Employer holds the balance after the Performance Certificate.** This is a direct breach of the mechanism; the Contractor records the breach and, where necessary, moves to a Sub-Clause 20.2 claim.

The cumulative effect of these mistakes is that retention becomes **free credit**: the Contractor in effect finances its Employer for the entire DNP and beyond. In MDB projects and long DNP this is a material amount of working capital frozen without interest. The prevention — an accurate retention calculation on every interim valuation and a fixed claim to interest. ## How retention money differs from performance security

<div class="bc-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Parameter</th><th>Retention Money</th><th>Performance Security</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>What it is</td><td>A percentage withheld from each payment</td><td>A bank guarantee or collateral (usually 10% of the price, exact value in the Contract Data)</td></tr><tr><td>Timing</td><td>Returned after Taking-Over and after DNP</td><td>Submitted within 28 days of the Letter of Acceptance (Sub-Clause 4.2)</td></tr><tr><td>Function</td><td>Incentive to rectify defects during DNP</td><td>Security for the performance of the Contractor's obligations</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

Retention Money and **Performance Security** cover different risks and different phases. Performance Security (Sub-Clause 4.2) is a security at the execution phase, which the Contractor submits within 28 days of receiving the Letter of Acceptance. Retention Money is a mechanism at the defects-liability phase: it is returned only after Taking-Over and the expiry of the DNP. In most contracts both instruments coexist and add up for the Contractor as “frozen capital”. If the Employer refers to both retention and security in a single demand, it is important for the Contractor to calculate precisely what amount has been withheld and what has been posted as collateral. Related terms and their role in the dispute mechanism are covered in Notice of Claim under FIDIC.

## Related terms and where to see the details

- **Defects Notification Period (DNP)** — the warranty period after which the balance of the retention is returned.
- **Taking-Over Certificate** — the event after which the first half of the retention is returned.
- **Performance Certificate** — issued under Sub-Clause 11.9; after its issue the Contractor treats its obligations as discharged.
- **Delay Damages** and **EOT** — adjacent calculation mechanisms in Clause 8; do not confuse them with retention, but they do intersect in payment calculations.
- **Notice of Claim** — if the Contractor disagrees with the Engineer's decision on the return, the dispute proceeds under 20.2.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is retention money under FIDIC in simple terms?

It is a percentage withheld from each interim payment that the Employer keeps as security for the rectification of defects. The return happens in two stages — after the Taking-Over Certificate is issued and after the DNP expires. The standard limit is 5% up to 50% of the Works and 0% beyond that; the exact value is in the Contract Data.

### When does the contractor get the retention back under FIDIC?

The first half of the retained amount — after the Taking-Over Certificate is issued (Sub-Clause 14.9 in 2017; 14.10 in 1999). The balance — after the Defects Notification Period expires and the Performance Certificate is issued under Sub-Clause 11.9.

### What is the standard retention percentage under FIDIC?

The typical limit is 5% of the accumulated value of Works executed up to 50% of the price, and 0% beyond that threshold. The ceiling of the retention is 10% (a Contract Data value). If the Particular Conditions have changed it, the amended value applies.

### How does retention money differ from performance security?

Retention Money is a withholding from each payment returned in two stages after DNP; Performance Security is a guarantee or collateral (usually 10% of the price, exact value in the Contract Data) that the Contractor submits within 28 days of the Letter of Acceptance under Sub-Clause 4.2. They cover different phases and risks and usually operate together.
