Advance Warning is an early warning of a future risk to the project. The FIDIC 2017 edition introduced it in Sub-Clause 8.4: each Party and the Engineer must tell the others in advance of known or probable events that may adversely affect the work, increase the Contract Price or delay completion.

> **In brief:** Advance Warning is the duty under Sub-Clause 8.4 of FIDIC 2017 to warn in advance of an event that may affect the work, the price or time. It is a risk-management tool, not a claim: only a Notice of Claim given within 28 days preserves the entitlement to time and money. The 1999 edition has no such duty.

## What to warn about, and how

- Events that may adversely affect the work of the Contractor's Personnel.
- Anything that may adversely affect the performance of the Works when completed.
- Anything that may increase the Contract Price.
- Anything that may delay the execution of the Works or a Section.
- The warning is given by whoever learns of the risk first: the Contractor, the Employer or the Engineer.

<aside class="bc-note"><strong>Look at your own contract first</strong><p>The time limits and procedure here follow the FIDIC 2017 General Conditions. The Contract Data and Particular Conditions may change them, so check the text of your own contract.</p></aside>

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there an Advance Warning in the 1999 edition?

No. It is a new obligation of the 2017 edition.

### Does an Advance Warning preserve the right to claim?

No. If the risk materialises, a separate Notice of Claim must be given within 28 days.
