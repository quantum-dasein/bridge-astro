An Engineer’s Determination is the Engineer's decision on a matter the Parties could not agree: the amount of a claim, an extension of time, the valuation of a Variation. In the FIDIC 2017 edition the procedure is in Sub-Clause 3.7, and the Engineer must act neutrally in it, not as the Employer's representative.

> **In brief:** Under Sub-Clause 3.7 of FIDIC 2017 the Engineer first helps the Parties to agree: 42 days are allowed for agreement. If there is none, the Engineer makes a determination within the following 42 days. A dissatisfied Party gives a Notice of Dissatisfaction within 28 days; otherwise the determination becomes final and binding.

## How the Sub-Clause 3.7 procedure runs

1. Consultation: the Engineer discusses the matter with both Parties and encourages agreement.
2. Agreement: if the Parties agree within 42 days, the Engineer records the agreement.
3. Determination: if they do not, the Engineer makes a reasoned determination within the following 42 days.
4. Compliance: the Parties give effect to the agreement or determination even if one of them disagrees.
5. Dissatisfaction: a NOD is given within 28 days; the dispute may then be referred to the DAAB.

<aside class="bc-note"><strong>Look at your own contract first</strong><p>The time limits and procedure here follow the FIDIC 2017 General Conditions. The Contract Data and Particular Conditions may change them, so check the text of your own contract.</p></aside>

## What changed from the 1999 edition

In the 1999 edition determinations are in Sub-Clause 3.5: the Engineer makes a fair determination, but without step-by-step time limits and without a separate notice-of-dissatisfaction regime. The 2017 edition added strict periods and expressly requires the Engineer to be neutral — one of the main differences between the two editions.

## Common mistakes

- A Party disagrees with the determination but gives no NOD within 28 days, and loses the chance to dispute it.
- The determination is not complied with "until the dispute is decided", although it binds at once.
- The Employer tries to dictate the content of the determination to the Engineer.
- A Party holds documents back during consultation, hoping to "save" them for arbitration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an Engineer’s Determination binding?

Yes. The Parties must give effect to it at once. If neither Party gives a NOD within 28 days, it becomes final and binding.

### Does the Engineer act for the Employer when making a determination?

No. Under Sub-Clause 3.7 of the 2017 edition the Engineer acts neutrally between the Parties and is not deemed to act for the Employer.
