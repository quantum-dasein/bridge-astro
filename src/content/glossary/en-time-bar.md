A time bar is a contract term under which an entitlement is lost if a party does not take a formal step within a stated period. In FIDIC contracts the main such period is the 28 days for the Notice of Claim. A claim may be entirely sound on the merits and still fail because the notice was missed.

> **In brief:** Under FIDIC 2017 the Notice of Claim is given no later than 28 days after the party became aware, or should have become aware, of the event (Sub-Clause 20.2.1). Missing the period puts the entitlement to time and money for that event at risk. The second key period is the 84 days for the fully detailed Claim (Sub-Clause 20.2.4).

## How to stay clear of the time bar

1. Keep an event register: what happened, when you became aware, which period is running.
2. When in doubt, give notice: it costs nothing, and its absence costs the claim.
3. Give the notice as a Notice, with a reference to the Sub-Clause.
4. Do not wait for final figures: the calculation comes later, with the substantiation.
5. Keep evidence of the date on which you became aware of the event.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a time bar in the 1999 edition?

Yes. Under Sub-Clause 20.1 of the 1999 edition the Contractor also gives notice within 28 days; otherwise time is not extended and no additional payment is due.

### Does the time bar apply to the Employer?

In the 2017 edition, yes: the Employer claims under the same Sub-Clause 20.2 and within the same period.
